Every tool here exists because it had a job to do. Each one processes your files on your own machine, at full quality, and tells you plainly what it can and cannot do.

Non-linear editor

A real timeline, not a trimmer.

Drag video, audio and stills onto layered tracks. Trim by the edge, split at the playhead, move clips between tracks, and watch the result compose live.

  • Overwrite editing — dropping a clip splits what it lands on, exactly as an editor should
  • Per-clip control — opacity, scale, position, rotation, speed, volume, fades
  • Frame-accurate export — every source seeked to an exact time, so renders never depend on machine speed

Restoration

Erase what should not be there.

Box the logo or the burned-in timestamp and choose how it goes: inpainted from the surrounding pixels, blurred, pixelated, or cropped away entirely.

  • Inpainting solves a Laplace fill across the hole — near-perfect over sky, blur and gradients
  • Honest about limits — over fine texture it smudges, and the tool says so rather than pretending
  • Crop removes the pixels outright when the background is too busy to fake
The original tool

Every frame, exactly.

Pull a clip apart into numbered JPEGs at any frame rate and download the lot as a single archive. The frames behind this page came out of it.

  • Any rate — 30 fps by default, or one frame every few seconds
  • Hand-written ZIP writer — no library, verified against Windows' own archive reader
  • Watermark removal can be baked into every frame as it is written
Documents

Papers, handled with the same care.

Merge anything into one PDF, join Word files, or convert between them — with structure preserved rather than flattened into a picture.

  • PDF to Word rebuilds paragraphs, runs, pictures and column-aligned tables as real Word objects
  • Position kept — the page size is copied and images are anchored to their original coordinates
  • Written against ECMA-376, validated by the same package reader Word itself uses

Craft

Nothing leaves your machine.

Not a marketing line — an architectural fact. There is no upload endpoint in this application. Decoding, processing and encoding all happen in your browser, which is why it still works with the network unplugged.
